How to read this

  • H (lead time) and W (window): an alert counts if it comes between H + W and H hours before a failure. H = 0, W = 48 means "within the 48 hours before the failure"; the failure hour itself is never scored.
  • Two rule sets. "H 0, W 48" is each rule exactly as notebook 04 kept it. "H 12, W 36" is the same three rules re-tuned for the recommended window: for each rule, the setting with the best F1 on the dev machines at H = 12, W = 36 (the settings are listed under the rule descriptions). Within a rule set the settings stay fixed as you change H, W or the event; each line is re-fitted from the hours that count as normal operation for that window. For a single part, C09 and C10 watch only that part's own sensor, error codes and age.
  • Precision: share of alerts followed by a failure within H + W. Coverage: share of failures with at least one alert in their window. F1 balances the two. Lift: how much more often the rule fires before failures than in normal running.
  • Why re-tune for H 12, W 36? With the rules as first kept, C09 scores F1 0.81 there: 20% of failures get their first alert only in the final 12 hours, which no longer counts. Re-tuned, C09 uses its machine's outlier-proof line (P99.9), which crosses earlier, and scores 0.94, a little higher than at H 0, W 48 (0.92). The write-up, section 11, has the details.
  • Alert lines on holdout machines are label-free: they come from all of that machine's own hours, never from its failure dates.
  • All numbers are on the 50 holdout machines, which never influenced any choice. Intervals resample whole machines (200 resamples). The data is synthetic and very clean, so treat absolute numbers as optimistic.
