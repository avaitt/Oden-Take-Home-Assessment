Rule explorer

See how the three kept rules score on the 50 holdout machines for any window (H, W) and any event. The chosen window is H = 12, W = 36: an alert counts if it comes between 48 and 12 hours before a failure, so every alert that counts leaves at least half a day to act.

C09 · Matched signature. Alert when, for any one part, its own sensor (6 h average) is more than 3.09 robust spreads from the machine's median in the fault direction (e.g. voltage above ~190, normal ~170), and that part's own error code was logged at least once in the last 24 h.
C08 · Drift + any error. Alert when any of the four sensors (6 h average) is more than 3.72 robust spreads from the machine's median in its fault direction, and any error code was logged at least once in the last 48 h.
C10 · Drift on an aging part. Alert when, for any one part, its sensor (6 h average) is more than 3.72 robust spreads from the machine's median in the fault direction, and the part has gone longer since its last replacement than its median in normal running (31–34 days).

Each part's own sensor and error codes: comp1 → voltage (rises), error1 · comp2 → rotation (falls), error2 or error3 · comp3 → pressure (rises), error4 · comp4 → vibration (rises), error5. The write-up, section 6, has the rules in full.

How to read this

  • H (lead time) and W (window): an alert counts if it comes between H + W and H hours before a failure. The failure hour itself is never scored.
  • The rules stay the same as you change H, W or the event. Each alert line is re-fitted from the hours that count as normal operation for that window. For a single part, C09 and C10 watch only that part's own sensor, error codes and age.
  • Precision: share of alerts followed by a failure within H + W. Coverage: share of failures with at least one alert in their window. F1 balances the two. Lift: how much more often the rule fires before failures than in normal running.
  • All numbers are on the 50 holdout machines, which never influenced any choice. A holdout machine's alert lines come from all of its own hours, never from its failure dates. Intervals resample whole machines (200 resamples). The data is synthetic and very clean, so treat absolute numbers as optimistic.
