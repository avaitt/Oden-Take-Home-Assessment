Machine explorer: watch C09 work on one machine

One machine's whole year on one chart: a sensor's daily reading, every breakdown, and every alarm C09 would have raised. Stay on the 50 test machines: the rule never saw them, so this is a fair preview of a new machine.

Look for 1Each red ✗ is a breakdown. Just to its left, a blue dot is C09 warning about it, about a day ahead.
Look for 2The grey line is the sensor for the part that breaks most on this machine. It steps up (or down, for rotation) right before each ✗.
Look for 3A hollow blue dot is a false alarm: no breakdown within the next 2 days. A breakdown weeks later doesn't count. Three in four machines have two or fewer all year.

This machine's breakdowns, and whether C09 warned in time

Alarms are not breakdowns

A breakdown (✗) is what actually happened: a part broke and was replaced. An alarm (●) is C09's warning that one is coming. A breakdown counts as caught if an alarm came 48 to 12 hours before it, and an alarm is real if a breakdown followed within 48 hours. One breakdown often gets two alarms, because the sensor can dip under the line and cross it again; both are the same warning.

2 days before   the part's sensor goes off its normal     no alarm yet: C09 also needs the error code
1 day before    the part's own error code is logged      alarm ●
18 h before     the sensor dips under and re-crosses     alarm ●  (a repeat)
breakdown       the part fails and is replaced           ✗  one breakdown, warned about 24 h ahead

Test machines played no part in building the rule; every choice was made on the other 50 (training) machines, which you can open with "All 100" for reference. Each part has one sensor that watches it (comp1 voltage, comp2 rotation, comp3 pressure, comp4 vibration), and that sensor shifts in the two days before the part fails.
